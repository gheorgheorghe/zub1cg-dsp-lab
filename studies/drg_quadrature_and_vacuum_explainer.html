100+ °/s of Quadrature & Gold Getter Vacuum Packages

Why high-aspect-ratio DRIE silicon Disk Resonator Gyroscopes naturally have huge mechanical quadrature, how tiny phase drift turns it into massive rate bias, and what happens inside hermetic ceramic LCC getter packages.

1 Live n = 2 Wineglass Mode: Why Quadrature Happens

A Silicon DRG vibrates in an n = 2 wineglass mode: the drive mode flexes along 0° / 90°, while Coriolis rotation transfers energy into the degenerate sense mode at 45° / 135°. Because DRIE etch taper, footing, and mask-to-crystal misalignment tilt the principal stiffness axes slightly, the drive force directly excites the sense electrodes—producing 50–500+ °/s of raw mechanical quadrature right off the wafer!

Look closely at the sense electrodes (red): With Ωz = 0 and Quad = 200 °/s, the ring still flexes strongly into the 45° sense axis—in-phase with drive displacement (90° out of phase with Coriolis velocity). Flip on DC Quad Electrodes or CLQ Nulling and watch the sense-axis motion collapse back to pure 0°/90° standing vibration!

2 Phasor Proof: How 0.1° of Phase Error Destroys Bias

In the rotating I/Q frame, Quadrature (SY) sits at 90° to Coriolis Rate (SX). An ideal lock-in ignores it—but any tiny demodulation phase error Δφ (from TIA op-amp temp drift, ADC skew, or PLL phase lag) rotates the axes and projects a giant shadow onto rate:

Ω_leak = Q_equiv · sin(Δφ)   |   200 °/s × sin(0.1°) = 0.349 °/s = 1,257 °/hr!

Key insight — Stiffness vs. Damping Asymmetry:
• Stiffness Quadrature (k_xy) is 90° out of phase → you can crush it by 40–80 dB with DC quad electrodes + FPGA CLQ.
• Anisodamping (g_xy) is 0° in-phase with Coriolis → no phase trim can separate it! It scales inversely with Q, which is why vacuum pressure and getter stability directly control long-term bias drift.

3 Why High-Aspect-Ratio DRIE Makes Quadrature Easy to Trigger

1. Sidewall Taper & Footing

Even 0.1°–0.3° of asymmetric Bosch-etch sidewall angle across 100+ µm deep spokes shifts local ring stiffness enough to rotate the wineglass axes by tenths of a degree → 100–400 °/s raw quadrature.

2. Crystal & Mask Alignment

Single-crystal silicon has anisotropic Young's modulus. On (100)-Si (or even slightly misaligned (111)-Si), a tiny mask-to-flat angle error directly couples the 0° and 45° modes.

3. Packaging Thermal Stress

Die-attach epoxy or AuSn eutectic lid sealing puts asymmetric strain into the silicon anchor. As temperature changes, that strain warps the stiffness tensor—drifting quadrature over temperature!

Why getters matter over years: Narrow high-aspect-ratio capacitive gaps (1.5–4 µm) create strong squeeze-film air damping above ~5–10 mTorr. A properly baked & activated NEG getter holds 10⁻³ to 10⁻⁵ Torr so air damping fades below TED. If organic die-attach outgasses or the getter saturates, pressure creeps right → Q drops, ring-up shortens, Brownian ARW climbs, and gas-driven anisodamping shifts rate bias!
