CVG Crash Course: Wineglass Gyros, DX/DY/SX/SY & ADEV

Everything you need to understand a 2D Disk Resonator Gyro (DRG) or Hemispherical Resonator Gyro (HRG) without drowning in differential equations — from why a vibrating ring senses rotation, to what every telemetry signal means, to reading an Allan Deviation bathtub like a pro.

1. Physics A Foucault Pendulum Shrunk Into a Wineglass

When you rub the rim of a crystal wineglass, it rings in an n = 2 oval standing wave — stretching horizontally, then vertically, 11,000 times per second (11 kHz). Because a ring or hemisphere is circularly symmetric, it actually has two identical vibration modes tilted 45° apart:

  • Drive Mode (x, 0° / 90°): We deliberately pump this mode so it oscillates continuously at a constant amplitude.
  • Sense Mode (y, 45° / 135°): When the gyro is sitting still, this 45° mode stays quiet (0).

Now spin the chip around its center at rate Ωz. Just like a Foucault pendulum keeps swinging in the same inertial plane while the Earth turns underneath it, the vibrating standing wave resists turning with the chip. From the chip's point of view, the Coriolis force transfers energy from the 0° Drive mode into the 45° Sense mode! The wave lags behind the shell by Bryan's Factor (Ag ≈ 0.28 for HRG, ~0.40 for DRG).

2. Demodulation The Strobe-Light Trick: What Are DX, DY, SX, SY?

The ring vibrates at 11,000 Hz — way too fast for a simple control loop to track directly. So the FPGA uses a Lock-In Demodulator: it multiplies the Drive (x) and Sense (y) pickoffs by a reference clock (sin φ and cos φ) and low-pass filters them.

Strobe-Light Intuition: Imagine filming an 11 kHz vibrating guitar string with an 11 kHz strobe light. The fast vibration "freezes" into slow, smooth DC numbers! Taking two strobe snapshots 90° apart (in-phase and quadrature) splits both axes into four crystal-clear health numbers:
DX
Drive In-Phase · "How Big Is the Swing?"

Measures the amplitude of the 0° Drive vibration. If DX drops, the ring is losing energy; if it grows, we're overdriving it.

Target: 1.000  |  Controlled by: AGC
DY
Drive Quadrature · "Is Our Timing Right?"

Measures phase error between our digital clock (NCO) and the ring's natural resonance. Like pushing a kid on a swing slightly too early or too late!

Target: 0.000  |  Controlled by: PLL
SX
Sense In-Phase · "Did Coriolis Push Us?"

Measures 45° Sense motion that is in-phase with Drive velocity — the exact signature of true rotation Ωz!

Target: 0.000 (rebalanced)  |  Controlled by: FTR
SY
Sense Quadrature · "Crooked-Spring Wobble"

Measures 45° Sense motion 90° out of phase with Coriolis — caused by tiny micron-level manufacturing imperfections coupling Drive straight into Sense.

Target: 0.000  |  Controlled by: CLQ

3. Control Loops The Bathroom-Scale Trick: PLLAdj, AGCAdj, CLQAdj, FTRAdj

Newcomers often ask: "Wait — if the controller forces SX = 0 all the time, how do we know how fast we're spinning?!"

Force-Rebalance Intuition: Think of holding a bowling ball perfectly still at chest height. Its position stays at 0 (SX = 0), so you don't measure how far it falls — you measure how hard your arms have to push up (FTRAdj) to hold it still! Every one of our four loop outputs is a free, calibrated physical sensor:
PLLAdj  (Hz)
Phase-Locked Loop → Built-In Thermometer!

Adjusts the NCO frequency so DY = 0 (staying dead-center on resonance). Because Silicon softens with heat (−30 ppm/°C = −0.33 Hz/°C), PLLAdj doubles as a millikelvin thermometer (we measured 0.006 °C RMS live on the ZUBoard!).

AGCAdj  (Force)
Automatic Gain Control → Vacuum / Q Health!

Pushes the Drive axis just enough to replace friction losses and hold DX = 1.0. Required force is ω0 / Q — so if the vacuum package ever leaks and Q drops, AGCAdj immediately rises to warn you.

CLQAdj  (Force)
Closed-Loop Quadrature → Symmetry Null!

Applies a 90°-phased counter-force to hold SY = 0, canceling 100+ °/s of fabrication spring-wobble so it can never leak into your rotation readout.

FTRAdj  (Force)
Force-to-Rebalance → THE GYRO RATE OUTPUT!

Applies an in-phase counter-force to cancel the Coriolis force and hold SX = 0. Since Coriolis force is 2 Ag DX Ωz, dividing FTRAdj by 2 Ag DX gives your exact rotation rate in °/s!

4. Noise & Drift Reading an Allan Deviation (ADEV) Bathtub Like a Pro

How do engineers grade a gyroscope? Put it completely stationary on a granite table for an hour, record its rate output Ω(t), and chop the recording into chunks of length τ (0.1 s, 1 s, 10 s, 100 s, 1000 s). Average each chunk, then measure how much adjacent chunk averages disagree with each other:

Every gyroscope ADEV forms a "bathtub" because short-term white noise averages away while long-term physical drift builds up.
LEFT WALL · SLOPE −1/2

Angle Random Walk (ARW)

Cause: Fast random thermal molecules kicking the resonator (Brownian noise) + front-end amplifier hiss.
Intuition: Averaging 4× longer cuts error by 2× (1/√τ). Higher Q and bigger DX pull this whole wall downward!

BOTTOM · SLOPE 0

Bias Instability (BI)

Cause: Slow 1/f flicker noise in electronics, residual quad phase jitter, and micro-fluctuations in damping.
Intuition: The flat bottom where averaging stops helping — the headline grade spec (Tactical ~0.1 °/hr, Nav-Grade ≤ 0.01 °/hr).

RIGHT WALL · SLOPE +1/2 (or +1)

Rate Random Walk (RRW) & Drift

Cause: True bias wandering away over minutes/hours due to temperature gradients, package solder stress, and drifting anisodamping Δ(1/τ).
Intuition: Like a drunkard's walk, waiting longer lets the zero-point wander farther (∝ √τ; linear thermal ramp gives slope +1).

The #1 Newcomer Gotcha — "Where Is Constant (DC) Bias on an ADEV Plot?!"
It isn't there! Because Allan Deviation subtracts adjacent window averages (Ωk+1 − Ωk), a fixed DC offset (even +50 °/hr!) cancels out to exact zero. Factory calibration can subtract a constant bias once — what kills inertial navigation is bias that refuses to stay constant (BI at the bottom + RRW on the right wall), which is why ADEV isolates the uncalibratable drift!
