Capacitive Transducer Physics · Push-Pull vs. Single-Ended

Differential Electrode Pairs in MEMS DRG & HRG

Why opposing push-pull pairs (+ / −) — and 4-quadrant 0°/180° vs. 90°/270° wiring — are mandatory for navigation-grade gyros, what happens with real DRIE gap mismatch (δ), and what our ZUBoard 1CG simulation models today vs. what an explicit gap-transducer upgrade adds.

Sim Status Does Our ZUBoard 1CG Sim Have Differential Electrode Pairs?

Short answer: Implicitly YES (ideal), Explicitly NO (not yet as individual nonlinear gaps).

Both our Python golden model (model/mems_gyro_model.py) and our 48-bit FPGA RTL plant (rtl/mems_hil/mems_plant_2dof.sv) operate directly in clean linear modal coordinates (x, y) with bipolar linear modal accelerations (ux, uy) and linear pickoffs (xq22, yq22).

Physically, that means our current sim assumes perfectly matched (δ = 0%) differential push-pull pairs — where DC pull force (Vp²), quadratic FTR distortion (vd²), even capacitive distortion (x²), common-mode feedthrough, and n=1 linear shock have already cancelled to zero! Adding an explicit d+(x), d−(x) capacitive transducer wrapper in Python lets us inject real single-ended distortion or finite DRIE etch mismatch (δ = 0.5–2.5%).

The Math Why Push-Pull Kills Every Even-Order Garbage Term

On an n=2 wineglass resonator, when the 0°/180° rim moves outward (+x, shrinking gap d+ = d0 − x), the 90°/270° rim moves inward by the exact same amount (−x, widening gap d− = d0 + x). Applying +vd on one side and −vd on the other gives:

Single-Ended Force:   F+ = (ε0A / 2d0²) · [Vp² + 2Vpvd + vd²] · [1 + 2(x/d0) + 3(x/d0)² + 4(x/d0)³ + …]
  → Contaminated by DC pull (Vp²), quadratic FTR bow (vd²), asymmetric x², and Vp ripple!
Differential Force (F+ + F−):   ALL even terms (Vp², vd², x², δVp) CANCEL IDENTICALLY!
Fdiff = (2ε0A Vp / d0²) · vd  +  (2ε0A Vp² / d0³) · x  +  O(x³)   (pure odd symmetry + 2× gain!)

Why 2-Electrode Differential Isn't Enough for Shock

A 2-electrode pair (0° minus 90°) cancels x², vd², and n=0 common-mode feedthrough — but does NOT cancel n=1 lateral shock (shaking the package sideways pushes the 0° wall closer while leaving 90° untouched!).

Why 4-Quadrant Wiring Cancels n=1 Shock Too!

Wire 0° + 180° together as Node + and 90° + 270° together as Node −! For the n=2 gyro mode, 0° and 180° move in phase (signals ADD!). Under lateral n=1 shock, 0° shrinks while 180° widens (shock CANCELS inside each node!).
