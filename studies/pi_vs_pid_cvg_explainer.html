Wait: PI, but no D?

Why Coriolis Vibratory Gyroscope (DRG/HRG) loops skip the derivative term in feedback—where the derivative actually lives—and how mechanical quadrature leaks into rate.

1 Demodulation Collapses 2nd-Order → 1st-Order

In the lab frame, a silicon DRG or fused-silica HRG is a stiff 2nd-order harmonic oscillator vibrating at 11 kHz:

ẍ + (ω₀/Q) ẋ + ω₀² x = F(t)

Once the I/Q lock-in demodulator strips off the 11 kHz carrier, you are only controlling the slow baseband envelopes (DX, DY, SX, SY). Factoring out the carrier collapses the dynamics to a clean 1st-order plant:

2 · d(DX)/dt + γₓ · DX = AGCAdj                   (Drive Amplitude) 2 · d(SX)/dt + γᵧ · SX = FTRAdj − 2·Ag·DX·Ωz   (Sense Coriolis Rate) 2 · d(SY)/dt + γᵧ · SY = CLQAdj − kₓᵧ·DX        (Sense Quadrature)
Why PI is already optimal here: At high Q (100k–5M), γ/2 = ω₀/(2Q) is tiny (~0.03–0.35 rad/s), so the demodulated plant looks like a single integrator (only 90° of phase lag, never 180°).
• P sets closed-loop bandwidth directly (ω_cl ≈ Kp / 2) with ~90° phase margin.
• I eliminates steady-state DC error (zero bias under constant rate, quadrature, or thermal drift).
• D isn't needed for stability—and as you can test below, adding it hurts!

3 Where the Derivative Actually Lives: Feedforward!

Instead of putting a noisy derivative inside the closed-loop actuator path, we invert the exact 1st-order sense equation outside the loop (see model/mems_gyro_model.py and host/gyro_dashboard.py):

Ω_FF(t) = [ FTRAdj − γᵧ · SX − 2 · d(SX)/dt ] / (2 · Ag · DX)

And when you integrate rate to get angle, the derivative cancels analytically into a purely algebraic, zero-noise-gain correction:

θ_FF(t) = ∫ [ (FTRAdj − γᵧ · SX) / (2 · Ag · DX) ] dt − 2 · SX / (2 · Ag · DX)
Best of both worlds: The FPGA feedback loop stays calm, low-noise, and rock-solid (pure PI), while Feedforward recovers the instantaneous transient rate and slashes integrated angle error by >10× with zero actuator noise amplification.

5 Bonus: Real-World DRG Quadrature Leakage

6 When Do You Actually Use D?

Control Loop Plant Order Controller Why
CVG AGC / FTR / CLQ 1st-order envelope PI + Feedforward 90° plant lag; D amplifies pickoff noise & 2f₀ ripple
